# Surface brief: Open-DTF landing (site/index.html)

Mode: Persuade. Build path: code-led (no image generation in this session).

## Direction contract

THESIS: The page is a press-check sheet: the proof a designer pulls before a print run. Open-DTF's job (turning tone into dots, plate by plate) is the sheet's own subject, so the page shows its separations instead of describing them. Refuses: dark SaaS product page with a centred headline, a blue accent and a row of equal cards.

OWN-WORLD: Cool coated-white stock (#f5f6f4, not cream), black K ink (#0b0b0c), saturated process inks as page-scale fields (cyan #00aeef, magenta #ec008c, yellow #ffe600). Crop marks and registration targets drawn in SVG/CSS, a CMYK colour bar as divider, tiny slug lines in the margins (real facts only: version, licence, 30 LPI, 33 deg). Type: Bricolage Grotesque for display, Archivo (width axis) for text and slug labels, Instrument Serif italic only for "Open" in the mark. The three mark dots become C, M, Y.

STORY: A designer understands Open-DTF is the free, open way to make halftones for DTF/DTG, sees the five steps as four plates plus the composite proof, believes it because the mechanism is shown and the licence is MIT, and downloads.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Full sheet: crop marks at the corners, registration target top right, slug line along the top edge. Headline set huge bottom-left in K, overprinting the live 3D halftone poster (top right) with its frequency/angle/knockout controls as a plate annotation. Primary action is a solid K plate button with a cyan registration offset, beside "view the code"; a colour bar closes the viewport.

FORM: Press-check sheet, candidate 1 of 7 on my ordered list (chosen by the user over the assigned step-wedge direction), seed: seed:d5c60b901b337816

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
